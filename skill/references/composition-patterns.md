# Composition Patterns

Notion-style illustrations are not metaphors or diagrams. They show **a person doing an
ordinary thing**, and let the caller supply the meaning. Resist the urge to be clever.

## The four asset types

| Type | Canvas | What it is | Default format |
|---|---|---|---|
| `illustration` | 1:1 square | One or two figures doing something | PNG or SVG |
| `character` | 1:1 square | One figure, neutral or posed, for reuse | SVG |
| `icon` | 24x24 grid | A single object, monoline | SVG |
| `spot` | 1:1 small | A tiny object or mark, no figure | SVG |

## Composition patterns for illustrations

**Single actor.** One person, one action, centred. The workhorse — use it unless there is a
reason not to. Reading, carrying, typing, watering a plant, locking a door.

**Pair, interacting.** Two figures sharing one object or one focus. Give them opposite
value schemes (one white top, one black top) so they separate. They should overlap slightly
— never stand apart like a lineup.

**Figure and oversized object.** The person is dwarfed by the thing they are dealing with:
a huge envelope, a giant clock, a stack of books taller than they are. The only permitted
exaggeration in the style, and it works well.

**Figure in a shape.** The person sits inside or emerges from a simple large form — a
circle, an arch, a rounded rectangle — which is filled with ~20% ink. Good for avatars and
section headers.

**Mid-stride.** A person walking with a trailing leg and a swinging arm, carrying
something. Reads as progress or transition. Very characteristic of the style.

## Composition rules

- **One action.** If you need "and" to describe it, it is two illustrations.
- **Centre the subject** with a 12-20% margin on all four sides.
- **One or two solid black masses**, usually hair and legwear, to anchor the value.
- **Overlap** figures and props rather than spacing them out.
- **Nothing touches the canvas edge.** Ever.
- **No ground line.** Figures float on nothing; a small scatter of accent dots near the feet
  implies the ground.
- Six to twelve accent marks, scattered asymmetrically.

## Things that quietly ruin it

- Adding a desk, floor, wall, or window to "ground" the scene
- Spacing two figures apart symmetrically
- Filling garments with grey instead of leaving them white
- Drawing every finger
- Making the head too large — this is not a chibi style
- Adding a caption or label inside the artwork
